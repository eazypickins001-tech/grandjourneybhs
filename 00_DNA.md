**The single biggest finding:** *No Baltimore behavioral-health provider runs a consistent, premium, faceless video channel.* Local accounts sit under 3.1K followers, post sporadically, and rely on quote cards, memes and stock photos. The local search intent — "Baltimore therapist", "what happens in therapy", "does Medicaid cover this" — is not served by video from anyone local.

**The second finding:** *National scale in this niche comes from volume plus one of two formats: a therapist talking head (Kati Morton, Therapy in a Nutshell) or animated listicles (Psych2Go).* Both are non-local and non-clinic. A clinic wins the local trust niche by pairing cinematic, calm visuals with plain-language access information nobody local is publishing.

## 3. The mechanics everyone in this niche shares

1. One emotion or one question per post — anxiety, burnout, grief, "am I normal".
2. Second-person address: "you", "your family", "your teen".
3. Symptom lists and checklists ("5 signs…").
4. Authority borrowed from licensure: "as a therapist…".
5. Anxiety, depression and trauma as the default topics; substance use and PRP are almost absent.
6. Text-first hooks designed for muted viewing.
7. Talking head, generic sad stock b-roll, or animated explainers.
8. A link-in-bio funnel to a directory, course or app.
9. Volume: daily to several posts a week.

**Ours:**

1. One concrete question per post — "what actually happens when you call?"
2. Second person, but about the *process*, not the diagnosis.
3. Steps and plain definitions instead of symptom lists.
4. Authority from the clinic itself: named services, real intake steps, real phone line.
5. PRP, IOP/PHP and substance use treatment first — the least-understood services.
6. Text built for mute, but the visual carries the metaphor.
7. Cinematic AI objects and empty rooms in the brand palette; real staff only with consent.
8. A phone call as the funnel: 833-888-0805, with the front desk as the human handoff.
9. Sustainable volume with a clinical review gate — accuracy over speed.

## 5. Format spec (do not deviate)

- **Length:** 22–35 s social; 40–60 s story.
- **Cadence:** 1 story post + 4 shorts per week (launch: 3 per week).
- **Clip limit:** Omni 10 s · Veo 8 s · this project uses **6 s default, 8 s max**.
- **Structure:**
  - `HOOK` — the interrupt, no logo, no "hey guys"
  - `SETUP` — identity, stakes
  - `TURN` — the mechanism or the real process
  - `TURN 2` — escalation or the part nobody says out loud
  - `PAYOFF` — the reveal, delivered
  - `CLOSE + CTA` — meaning, then one line
- **Visual grammar:** AI for metaphor, atmosphere, objects and empty rooms. Real footage only for real GJBHS spaces and consented staff; never for a client story. Text, steps and numbers are built in the editor, never generated.
- **Text:** always in the editor, never from the model.
- **Series:** every post belongs to a named series so the catalogue compounds.

## 7. Integrity guardrails (project-specific additions)

The universal five are in `_SHARED/prompt-formula.md` §7. On top of those:

1. **No client depiction, ever.** No patient, family member or identifiable person in distress — not AI-generated, not stock. Use objects, empty rooms, hands, silhouettes at most. Real staff appear only with signed consent and only in positive contexts.
2. **No fabricated proof.** Never invent testimonials, reviews, success rates, before/after outcomes or accreditation visuals. CARF accreditation is shown only as the real licensed mark, sourced from GJBHS.
3. **No diagnosis, no individualized advice.** Every symptom-adjacent post is framed as education and carries: "Educational only, not medical advice" plus the crisis line (call or text 988).
4. **HIPAA.** No protected health information anywhere — captions, comments, DMs, screenshots. Never confirm or deny that someone is a patient; route to 833-888-0805.
5. **Crisis-safe imagery.** No depiction of self-harm, substance use in progress, overdoses or methods. Resources, not scenes.
6. **Verify every service claim.** Medicaid acceptance, CARF status, hours and programs must be confirmed with the front office before publishing — the website is a starting point, not proof.

## 8. What this channel will never do

1. Fear-based or shock hooks about symptoms ("this could kill you").
2. "5 signs you have [diagnosis]" framing — we say "5 things worth talking about".
3. Trending audio, dances or meme formats that trivialize care.
4. Fake client stories or AI-generated testimonials.
5. A sales CTA on every post — one conversion post in five.
6. Promise outcomes ("you will recover in 30 days").
